Member Brief — Molloy Roofing Company

July onboarding call · Strategist: Feli · Sales: Johno

Who is who

  • Kentin Hare — the owner. Bought Molloy in Dec 2024 through his holding company, Cedar Ivy Group. Lives in Cincinnati.
  • Jennifer Hare — Kentin's partner. Runs the books on his other business (contracting / concrete).
  • Brian Holtman — third operator. Was on the first call with Feli. Role still unclear, but he's in the room.

What's the deal

Kentin bought a 125-year-old roofing company at the end of 2024. Great team. Lots of long-tenured staff. The previous accounting manager retired right when he took over. Kentin and Jennifer are doing too much by hand. They want help — start with one junior person, then more.

Already covered — don't re-ask

  • The acquisition story (Dec 2024, through Cedar Ivy)
  • What Cedar Ivy does (long-hold acquirer, family businesses, Cincinnati)
  • Their preference: start junior, easy to spoon-feed
  • Sagan pricing + model

Where to focus

The long-tenured Molloy staff are both the asset and the constraint. Understand who they are and what they own before suggesting hires. Anyone Sagan places lands inside that team.

Questions worth asking

Block 2 (their story):

  • "Working with the existing Molloy team since the acquisition — what's surprised you?"

Block 3 (the business):

  • "What's the part of running Molloy that's been harder than you thought?"

Block 4 (the team):

  • "Outside you and Jennifer, who at Molloy could you not run the business without?"
  • "What did the previous accounting manager actually own, day to day?"

Block 5 (roadmap):

  • "You said you want to start junior. What does success look like at month 6 with that first person?"

Don't do this

  • Don't bring back the 4-hire roadmap. He didn't engage with it last time.
  • Don't make Brian feel sidelined — he was on the first call. Address him directly.
  • Don't pitch. The relationship is the product.
  • Don't reference the sales transcript directly. Use the signal, don't show your homework.
