# Account Partner Onboarding Call — v2

**Total length:** up to 45 min

This isn't a kickoff. It's a **partnership intake**. The relationship that comes out of it is worth a 12-month retention curve, not a one-time placement. The discipline is to make every minute count — depth, not length.

---

## The philosophy shift from v1

| v1 (still vendor-flavored) | v2 (real partner) |
|---|---|
| Bond → role discovery → budget → process | **Bond → their story → the business → the team → role → budget → roadmap** |
| First hiring question at minute 3 | **First hiring question at minute 20+** |
| AP gets the brief, leaves | **AP knows the founder's "why," not just the JD** |
| Member feels: efficient | **Member feels: heard** |

The AP's job in the first 30 minutes is not to gather info. **It's to make the founder feel like they finally have someone who actually wants to understand the business.**

---

## Block 1 — Real Opening (4–6 min)

**Goal:** not "warm-up." Real human contact.

**What it looks like:**

- AP references one specific personal thing from the briefing (the kid mentioned in the sales call, the cowboys tee, the recent press hit, the marathon they ran)
- AP shares something equally personal back, unprompted. *"I'm calling from Mendoza, just got back from a rescue on the mountain Wednesday — had to find a friend who got lost in a storm. So you're getting me on day three of recovery."* Specific. Human. Real.
- Riff on whatever comes up. Don't rush to the agenda.
- Let the founder set the pace. Some founders are crisp; the bonding stays light. Some founders are open; give them a few extra minutes — but recover the time in later blocks.

**Rule:** do not say "let's get into it" until the founder has laughed once or shared one thing about themselves they didn't have to share. That's the signal it's safe to move forward.

**The AP also says, early in this block:**

> "I want to give you something different than what most recruiters do. Before I ask you about who you want to hire, I want to actually understand your business. So we're going to spend the first half of this call on that. Then we'll get to the hire. That cool with you?"

This single sentence resets the founder's expectations. Most of them have never been asked this. Most will say *"yeah, that's actually great."* Now you have permission to go deeper.

---

## Block 2 — Their Story (8–12 min)

**Goal:** understand the founder. Not the company yet. **The person.**

The Account Partner is not a recruiter in this block. They're a curious friend. The questions are open-ended and the AP listens 3x more than they talk.

**Anchor question:**

> "Walk me through how this started. What were you doing before, and what made you go all in on this?"

**Follow-on questions** (use as needed — don't run through them like a checklist):

- "Who was the first customer who made you realize this was a real business, not just an idea?"
- "What part of this work gives you energy? What part drains you?"
- "What did you think this business would be when you started, vs. what it actually became?"
- "What's the conversation you have with your spouse / your friends about this business that you don't have with your team?"
- "If you had to teach someone what it's actually like to run this kind of company in 10 minutes, what's the part nobody warns them about?"

These are not recruitment questions. They are **founder-respect questions**. Most founders will go deeper than the AP expects, because they've never been asked.

**Take notes. Not on Zoom. On paper. Where the founder can see you doing it. It matters.**

---

## Block 3 — The Business Itself (7–10 min)

**Goal:** understand the company without referencing hiring once.

By now the founder is warmed up and talking. Now we go into the business mechanics, but still through their lens, not ours.

**Anchor:**

> "Help me understand the actual business. If I walked into your office on a normal Tuesday, what's happening?"

**Follow-ons:**

- "Who are your customers, really? Not the marketing version. The actual person who buys."
- "What's the unfair advantage you have that your competitors can't copy?"
- "Where are you growing fastest right now? What's pulling you forward?"
- "What's harder than you thought it would be? Where is the friction?"
- "What's the part of your industry's conventional wisdom that you think is just wrong?"
- "If you could remove one constraint from the business tomorrow, which one?"
- "Where do you want this company in three years? What's the picture in your head?"

That last question is the bridge. It naturally pulls toward *"what do you need to get there"* — which is the gentle on-ramp to the hiring conversation. **But not yet.**

---

## Block 4 — The Team Today (5–7 min)

**Goal:** understand the org as it exists. **No hiring talk yet.**

> "Tell me about your team. Who's in the room with you?"

**Follow-ons:**

- "Who reports to who? Who's been there longest?"
- "Who's the person you couldn't run the business without — outside of yourself?"
- "Where's the team firing on all cylinders? Where is it stretched?"
- "What's the rhythm of the week look like? Standups? All-hands? Async?"
- "How does the team communicate? What tools? What's working, what isn't?"

This block is where the founder will **organically tell you their hiring problem**. They'll say things like *"Sarah does invoicing but she's drowning"* or *"my estimators are out in the field and the commercial bids pile up."* That's gold. You don't need to ask *"so what do you want to hire"* — they're already telling you.

When they get close to articulating a need, your job is to **slow down and listen, not pounce**.

---

## Block 5 — Personality, Roadmap, Growth Lever (6–8 min)

**This is where the AP earns the consultant title.**

- "Do you want someone who follows your lead exactly, or someone who pushes back?"
- "In 12 months, do you see this seat managing people, owning a function, or staying IC?"
- **The key question:** *"You're hiring this seat now. Tell me what the next seat is. And the one after that."*

The roadmap question is **the renewal anchor**. It is the entire reason the Account Partner role exists.

If the founder doesn't have a roadmap yet, the AP offers one: *"Based on what you just told me about [business growth direction], here's what I'd consider — your second hire is probably X, your third is probably Y. Does that fit your head?"*

That moment — where the AP shows up with a hypothesis the founder hadn't articulated — is when the relationship locks in.

---

## Block 6 — Co-Design + Close (3–5 min)

**Goal:** leave the founder feeling like they made a friend who's now their growth partner, not someone who just sold them a service.

**The roadmap commitment:**

> "Within 24 hours, you're getting a doc from me. Not a recap. A 90-day plan for hire #1 and a strategic frame for what comes next. We'll get on a 30-min call at day 30 to revisit and adjust. By month 6, we'll have hire #2 on the table. By month 11, we're already talking about year 2."

**Operational close:**

- Confirm next concrete step (first drop date — pick a slot)
- Schedule 30-day strategic check-in on the call
- Introduce TA by name and tenure
- Ask one more time: *"Anyone else who should be looped into our strategic conversations? Co-founder, CFO?"*

**Final close — warm and personal. Not corporate.**

> "Look — I'm going to make sure your hire lands well. But more than that, I want you to feel like you finally have someone who actually understands your business. Six months from now, you should look back at this call as the moment things shifted. That's the bar I'm setting."

---

## What's deliberately not in this call

Two blocks from earlier drafts are intentionally **left out** of the onboarding call:

| Left out | Where it happens instead |
|---|---|
| **Role mechanics** (day-to-day, tech stack, reports-to, customer-facing, "great at 90 days") | Handled by the TA in a separate role-intake call after the AP onboarding. The TA needs this granularity; the AP doesn't need it in the first conversation. |
| **Budget + process logistics** (rate confirmation, drop cadence, guarantee window, payment) | Handled before close by sales, or in a short follow-up by the AP / Decision Partner. Keeps the onboarding call focused on relationship + strategy. |

This keeps the onboarding call **pure**: bonding, story, business, team, roadmap, close. Operational detail comes later.

---

## The reading-the-room rule

The blocks above are a guide, **not a script**.

- If the founder is crisp and business-only, run the call faster (30–35 min). They'll signal it.
- If the founder opens up in block 1 and starts telling you about their kid's soccer game, **let them**. The bond is the business. Spend the time.
- If they keep pulling toward *"so when can you send candidates,"* the AP gently redirects: *"I'm getting there, but I want to do this right. Five more minutes on the business, then we go deep on the hire. Better candidates come out of better context."*
- If the founder seems impatient about the long format, the AP can compress block 2 and block 3 into 8 min total. **The order is more important than the time.**

---

## What this changes operationally

For Manu, Feli, Miguel (and any future AP), here's the shift they need to internalize:

1. **You are not gathering requirements. You are building a relationship.** The requirements are a byproduct of the relationship.
2. **Curiosity is the skill, not closing.** The questions in blocks 2–4 are the most important thing the AP does in the entire month.
3. **The structured handoff fields still get filled out** — but they're filled out after the call from notes, not extracted live.
4. **Bonding doesn't end at minute 8.** It threads through the whole call. If the founder shares something personal in block 5, the AP follows.
5. **Long-term means long-term.** This call is the start of a relationship that should still be active 18 months from now.

## Before the call — Tool 16 generates the right kind of brief

The AP doesn't walk into this call cold. **Tool 16 · Member Intelligence Enricher** runs 24h before the call.

**Critically, the brief is built for *this* kind of call — relationship-first, not hiring-first.** That means the brief loads up on:

- **Personal hooks for Block 1** — kids, hobbies, marathons, press, podcasts, anything from the founder's public life that gives the AP a real opening
- **Founder story signal** — prior companies, where they grew up, what they were doing before this, the "why" behind starting the business
- **Business mechanics** — what the company actually does (in plain words), customers, growth signals, competitors
- **Team intel** — who else is on LinkedIn at the company, who they hired most recently, any leadership churn
- **Hypothesis for the roadmap** — based on similar Sagan members and industry signals, what does the AP think comes next?

The AP reviews this 15 min before the call. Walks in with three things ready:
1. **One specific personal opener** (the kid, the marathon, the press hit)
2. **One thing they personally want to share back** (Block 1 vulnerability)
3. **A roadmap hypothesis** to surface in Block 5 if the founder doesn't have one

## After the call — automation kicks in

**Tool 14 · AP Meeting Briefing + Roadmap Updater** runs automatically after the Granola transcript lands:

- Generates the **partnership briefing doc** (not a hiring summary — a relationship document)
- Captures everything the founder shared: their story, what energizes them, what drains them, business mechanics, team dynamics, the roadmap
- Updates `Members.hiring_roadmap` + `Members.ai_agents_roadmap`
- Creates Demand Signals records for the Confirmed + Probable hires from Block 5
- Posts the briefing into the Member Portal (visible to the member if AP chooses to share)
- Notifies Sourcing of new demand signals via Slack

**AP review:** 15 min after the call, AP reviews the auto-generated briefing, edits anything wrong, hits "Publish to Member Portal."

---

## Within 24 hours — the commitment doc

The promise made in Block 6 ("Within 24 hours, you're getting a doc from me") gets delivered as a **partnership commitment doc** published to the Member Portal:

- Founder's "why" in their own words
- 90-day plan for hire #1
- Strategic frame for hires #2 and #3
- The 30-day strategic check-in scheduled
- The AP's promise: *"That's the bar I'm setting."*

This doc is what the founder forwards to their co-founder. It's what makes them say *"Sagan is different."*
