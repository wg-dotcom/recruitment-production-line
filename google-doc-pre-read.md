# Recruitment as a Production Line

**Author:** Jesus
**For:** Leadership Team
**Pre-read for:** Wednesday brainstorm
**Goal of this doc:** Get us aligned on the model before we meet, so we can spend Wednesday on the hard parts.

---

## TL;DR

I want to turn recruitment into a production line. Four stages, each with one clear output. The output of one stage becomes the input of the next. Recruiters recruit, salespeople sell, candidate ops polishes, sourcing fills the funnel. No more TAs doing five jobs at once.

The win: we present in minutes, bottlenecks become visible, and we scale by managing throughput instead of managing the process itself.

---

## Why now

Today our TAs are sourcing, vetting, polishing, presenting, and managing the member relationship at the same time. That works when volume is low. It breaks when we try to scale. Quality drifts, delivery times are unpredictable, and we can't isolate what's slowing us down because everything is tangled together.

I've been thinking about this for a while. Walking around the city this week with the team here in Mendoza, the picture finally clicked. We need to treat this like a real operation: clear stages, clear outputs, clear handoffs.

---

## The framework: Sales & Operations Planning, applied to recruitment

We're not inventing this. We're adopting a 40-year-old supply chain discipline that's already transformed industries from manufacturing to fashion.

**Sales & Operations Planning (S&OP)** is how operationally serious companies match what customers will want with what the business can produce. A five-step cycle: demand planning, supply planning, aggregate planning, execution, performance review. Every modern supply chain runs on some version of it.

We've been running recruitment without this discipline. Each member request lands as a one-off, gets pushed through whoever has capacity, and we react to bottlenecks after they hurt us. S&OP turns that on its head: plan demand, build supply ahead of it, match the two with structured handoffs, review performance at the stage level.

### The S&OP cycle, mapped to us

| # | S&OP Phase | Our Recruitment Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Demand Planning | Member hiring roadmaps + Decision Partner demand signals |
| 02 | Supply Planning | Sourcing capacity + Candidate Ops throughput |
| 03 | Aggregate Planning | AI matching + stage capacity per workstream |
| 04 | Execution | The four parallel workstreams in motion |
| 05 | Performance Review | Stage-level metrics (time-to-first-presentation, etc.) |

### Business case: Dell's Build-to-Order revolution

In the 1990s, Dell rewrote PC manufacturing with one big idea: **stop keeping finished PCs in stock**. Instead, hold a safety stock of components and assemble each machine only when an order came in.

The structural parallel to what we're proposing is almost one-to-one:

- **Component safety stock** = our ready-to-present candidate database
- **Build-to-order assembly** = match-on-demand recruitment
- **Direct sales relationship** = the Decision Partner

**Results:**
- Inventory dropped from 32 days to 4 days (87% reduction)
- Delivery time fell from 6 weeks to 5 days
- Dell became the #1 PC maker by 2001

Dell's competitors weren't beaten on product or price. They were beaten on operating model. Same opportunity is open to us in recruitment. The agencies we compete with are stuck running the slow, tangled flow. We can build the production line they don't even see.

## The Model

Each stage has an input, a set of activities, and an output. The output of one stage is the input of the next. Stage 4 feeds back into Stage 1's strategy, closing the loop.

---

### Stage 1. Sourcing

**Input:** Sourcing Strategy. Role demand forecast, location and seniority targets, market intelligence, priority flags (urgent / standard / specialty). This input is shaped by Stage 4 feedback and account roadmaps.

**Activities (three sub-functions, one team):**
- **Active Pipelines:** Always-on sourcing for the roles we fill every month. EAs, CSRs, SDRs, Bookkeepers, Accountants. Volume game.
- **Assault Team:** Outbound for the hard stuff. Specialty roles, weird qualifications, niche tech. ML, data, engineering.
- **Strategic Sourcing:** Location strategy, seniority mapping, market intelligence. Feeds the other two.

**Output:** Raw candidate pool. Sourced profiles with basic data (name, role, location, source channel), tagged by role family and priority. Volume-focused. Not yet ready to present.

---

### Stage 2. Candidate Operations

**Input:** Raw candidate pool from Stage 1.

**Activities (turns a sourced candidate into a presentation-ready asset):**
- Polished resumes
- Badass intro videos
- Standardized presentation cards
- Confirms availability and salary expectations
- Keeps candidates warm between opportunities

**Output:** Ready-to-present database (the safety stock). Polished resume, intro video, confirmed availability, salary range, last-contact date, role tags for AI matching. Smaller than the sourcing pool. Every profile is presentation-grade.

When a request comes in, we don't start from zero.

---

### Stage 3. Recruitment / Talent Advisors

**Input:** Ready-to-present database from Stage 2, plus the incoming JD from the member.

**Activities (now the TAs can actually do recruiting):**
- AI-driven matching: JD against the ready-to-present database
- Build the presentation pages
- Own the operational / HR side of the candidate relationship
- HR-side connection with the member / customer (operational interface, not strategic)
- Candidate interview preparation (coach candidates ahead of member interviews)
- Support interviews with the member

**Output:** Shortlisted candidates + interview intelligence. top 20 vetted candidates per JD, presentation page link, match rationale, interview reports, and candidate insights that feed Stage 4's advisory conversation with the member.

No more TAs hunting for candidates, confirming availability, or polishing resumes. They grab from the safety stock and present.

---

### Stage 4. Decision Partners / Account Growth

**Input:** Shortlisted candidates from Stage 3, plus member context (hiring roadmap, back-office operations, growth trajectory).

**Activities (sales professionals, not recruiters):**
- Owns the strategic member relationship
- Knows the member's hiring roadmap, operations, growth trajectory
- Advises on hiring decisions ("this candidate fits your back-office better because you mentioned scaling X")
- Sommelier energy. Fighter pilot energy. The trusted decision partner.

**Output:** Placement, retention, account expansion. Hire confirmed, member roadmap updated, next hire identified.

**Feedback loop:** The "next hire identified" output flows back into Stage 1 as updated sourcing strategy. Account growth signals tell sourcing what to build pipelines for. The line keeps feeding itself.

It's easier to coach a salesperson into understanding recruiting than to coach a recruiter into selling. We've already proven that with White Glove.

---

### Note: White Glove exception

For White Glove customers, Stage 3 and Stage 4 are **led by the same White Glove Advisor**. The Advisor owns both the recruitment execution and the strategic decision-partner relationship for that account. The production line model still applies, but the handoff between stages 3 and 4 happens inside one head, not across two teams.

This keeps the White Glove experience tight and personal while still benefiting from Stages 1 and 2 (sourcing volume and candidate ops polish) feeding the same way as every other line.

## Handoff Contracts

Each handoff is a documented contract. No verbal pass-offs, no Slack DMs as the source of truth.

| From → To | What gets passed |
|---|---|
| Sourcing → Candidate Ops | Sourced profile + basic data + priority tag |
| Candidate Ops → Recruitment | Polished profile + confirmed availability + salary range |
| Recruitment → Decision Partners | top 20 matched candidates + presentation page + match rationale + interview reports + candidate insights |
| Decision Partners → Sourcing (loop) | Demand signal: next roles needed, by which member, by when |

---

## Why this works

1. **Simple roles.** Each team owns one output. No context-switching, no guessing what to do next.
2. **Bottlenecks become visible.** Slow presentations? That's Candidate Ops. Slow matching? That's Recruitment. Fix the stage without breaking the line.
3. **Speed without quality tradeoff.** Safety stock of pre-vetted candidates means we present in minutes, not days.
4. **AI-ready.** Each stage becomes more automatable as tools improve. The structure compounds with technology.
5. **Better candidate experience.** A team dedicated to polishing profiles means candidates put their best foot forward. The structure also filters out ghosters early. The cream rises.
6. **Right people, right seats.** Recruiters recruit. Decision Partners advise and close. Sourcers source. No more square pegs in round holes.

## Workstreams (build in parallel)

We don't need to finish one stage before starting the next. The four workstreams advance at the same time. The only hard dependency is that we can't fully execute the new model until **Candidate Operations is delivering**. That's the gate.

**Primary focus:** scoping the Decision Partner / Account Growth role well. It's the linchpin of the whole system.

## Infrastructure: the ready-to-present database

This is the safety stock that makes the whole line fast. If it isn't well built, nothing downstream is reliable. So it deserves its own section.

### Where it lives

One central system. Single source of truth. API-accessible so the AI matching tool, the presentation page builder, and the dashboards can all query it. No duplicates across spreadsheets, Notion pages, or recruiter heads.

**Strong candidate: Airtable.** We already use it, the MCP integration is in place, and it supports the field structure without custom code. Open to alternatives if someone proposes a better fit.

### Field structure (every Ready record must include)

Machine-readable, structured for AI matching:

- **Identity:** Name, location, country, time zone
- **Role tags:** Primary role + adjacent roles (multi-select)
- **Seniority + experience years**
- **Salary range:** Confirmed, in USD
- **Availability:** Status (active / paused) + last-confirmed date
- **Assets:** Polished resume URL + intro video URL
- **Tech / tools stack** (multi-select)
- **Language proficiency**
- **Source channel + sourced-by**
- **Lifecycle status:** Sourced / Polishing / Ready / Placed / Paused / Inactive

### Access model (who writes, who reads)

- **Sourcing** creates the initial record (Sourced status).
- **Candidate Ops** writes everything, owns promotion to Ready, owns the database. This is their responsibility.
- **Recruitment / TAs** read-only. Matches against the database and builds presentation pages from records. Cannot edit candidate data directly.
- **Decision Partners** read-only. Uses match output and interview intelligence to advise the member.

### Lifecycle

- **Entry:** Only Candidate Ops can promote a record to *Ready*. Promotion requires all required fields filled and both assets attached.
- **Refresh:** Weekly availability + salary check. Records not refreshed in 30 days auto-flag.
- **Decay:** 60 days no refresh → drops to *Paused*. 90 days → *Inactive*.
- **Reactivation:** Inactive records can re-enter the line via Candidate Ops only.
- **On placement:** Status flips to *Placed* with member + start date. Stays in the database for replacement scenarios.

### Recommendation

Build v1 in **Airtable**. Same base across all four workstreams. Same MCP integration we already run. We can layer a custom front-end later if Airtable's UI becomes the bottleneck, but the data model stays portable.

## The risks (and how we handle them)

I've already stress-tested this. Here's what could go sideways and what we do about it.

### Risk 1. The handoff tax

If handoffs are sloppy, the integration cost eats the efficiency gains. Today, one TA owns the candidate end-to-end. That's slow but it's seamless.

**How we handle it:**
- Define the contract between every stage. Each handoff has a documented input spec and output spec.
- Async-first, with a single shared system of record. No verbal handoffs, no Slack DMs as the source of truth.
- Stage SLAs. Example: Candidate Ops processes a sourced candidate within 48 hours. Recruitment matches within 24 hours of receiving a presentation-ready profile.
- Weekly pipeline review at the stage level, not at the request level.

### Risk 2. Losing the power of surfacing

Right now we have huge sourcing volume and we surface fast. The safety stock model means a smaller ready-to-present database. We can't lose that surfacing speed.

**How we handle it:**
- The ready-to-present database is fed continuously. Sourcing never slows down.
- Recruitment actually gets faster because they stop wasting time confirming availability or polishing profiles. They grab and go.
- For urgent or unique roles, the Assault Team fast-tracks through Candidate Ops with a priority flag.
- Metric to watch: time-to-first-presentation. Should decrease, not increase.

### Risk 3. The system can't depend on one person to run

If this requires my supply chain brain to operate every day, it isn't built right.

**How we handle it:**
- Each stage has a stage lead who owns throughput for that stage.
- We manage the system, not the requests. Monitor flow, unblock bottlenecks, optimize stage performance.
- A stage lead can onboard new people fast because the scope is narrow and trainable. We don't need unicorns at every seat.
