Recruitment as a Production Line

Four stages, four outputs, one feedback loop. A lean process where each team owns one job, bottlenecks are visible, and we scale by managing throughput instead of managing chaos.

Author: Jesus Pacheco
For: Leadership Team
Date: May 8, 2026
TL;DR

Today our TAs source, vet, polish, present, and manage member relationships at the same time. That works at low volume. It breaks when we scale. Quality drifts, delivery times are unpredictable, and bottlenecks are invisible because everything is tangled together.

The proposal: turn recruitment into a real production line. Recruiters recruit, salespeople sell, candidate ops polishes, sourcing fills the funnel. We present in minutes, bottlenecks become obvious, and we scale by managing the system instead of every request.

Framework

Sales & Operations Planning, applied to recruitment

We're not inventing this. We're adopting a 40-year-old supply chain discipline that's already transformed industries from manufacturing to fashion.

Sales & Operations Planning (S&OP) is how operationally serious companies match what customers will want with what the business can produce. A five-step cycle: demand planning, supply planning, aggregate planning, execution, performance review. Every modern supply chain runs on some version of it.

We've been running recruitment without this discipline. Each member request lands as a one-off, gets pushed through whoever has capacity, and we react to bottlenecks after they hurt us. S&OP turns that on its head: plan demand, build supply ahead of it, match the two with structured handoffs, review performance at the stage level.

Business case · It worked before

Dell's Build-to-Order revolution

In the 1990s, Dell rewrote PC manufacturing with one big idea: stop keeping finished PCs in stock. Instead, hold a safety stock of components and assemble each machine only when an order came in.

The structural parallel to what we're proposing is almost one-to-one:

Component safety stock = our ready-to-present candidate database.
Build-to-order assembly = match-on-demand recruitment.
Direct sales relationship = the Decision Partner.

Same logic, different industry. We're applying it to talent.

Why this matters for us
Dell's competitors weren't beaten on product or price. They were beaten on operating model. The same opportunity is open to us in recruitment. The agencies we compete with are stuck running the slow, tangled flow. We can build the production line they don't even see.
The Model

Four stages. One flow. A feedback loop.

Each stage takes a clear input, runs a focused set of activities, and produces a clear output. The output of one stage becomes the input of the next.

Stage 01
Sourcing
Input
Sourcing Strategy. Role demand forecast, location and seniority targets, market intel, priority flags. Shaped by Stage 4 feedback.
Activities
  • Active Pipelines (EAs, CSRs, SDRs, Bookkeepers, Accountants)
  • Assault Team (specialty / hard-to-fill roles)
  • Strategic Sourcing execution
Output
Raw candidate pool. Sourced profiles with basic data, tagged by role family and priority. Volume-focused.
Stage 02
Candidate Operations
Input
Raw candidate pool from Stage 1.
Activities
  • Polished resumes + intro videos
  • Confirms availability and salary
  • Standardized presentation cards
  • Keeps candidates warm
Output
Ready-to-present database. The safety stock. Polished profile, video, confirmed availability and salary, role tags for AI matching.
Stage 03
Recruitment / TAs
Input
Ready-to-present database from Stage 2, plus the incoming JD from the member.
Activities
  • AI matching: JD ↔ database
  • Builds presentation pages
  • Owns operational / HR relationship
  • Candidate interview preparation
  • Supports interviews with the member
  • HR-side connection with the member / customer
Output
Shortlisted, matched candidates + interview intelligence. top 20 vetted candidates per JD, presentation page, match rationale, interview reports, and candidate insights to inform Stage 4's advisory conversation.
Stage 04
Decision Partners / Account Growth
Input
Shortlisted candidates from Stage 3, plus member context (hiring roadmap, operations, growth trajectory).
Activities
  • Strategic member relationship
  • Knows hiring roadmap + operations
  • Advises on the hire (sommelier-level judgment)
  • Manages expansion + retention
Output
Placement, retention, account growth. Hire confirmed, roadmap updated, next hire identified.
↻ Demand Signal Feedback Loop
Stage 4's "next hire identified" output flows back into Stage 1's sourcing strategy. Account growth tells sourcing what to build pipelines for. The line keeps feeding itself.
WG
Note · White Glove Exception
For White Glove customers, Stage 3 and Stage 4 are led by the same White Glove Advisor. The Advisor owns both the recruitment execution and the strategic decision-partner relationship for that account. The production line still applies, but the handoff between stages 3 and 4 happens inside one head.
Handoff Contracts

The glue between stages

Each handoff is a documented contract. No verbal pass-offs, no Slack DMs as the source of truth.

From → To What gets passed
Sourcing → Candidate Ops Sourced profile + basic data + priority tag
Candidate Ops → Recruitment Polished profile + confirmed availability + salary range
Recruitment → Decision Partners top 20 matched candidates + presentation page + match rationale + interview reports + candidate insights
Decision Partners → Sourcing (loop) Demand signal: next roles needed, by which member, by when

Infrastructure

The ready-to-present database

The safety stock that makes the whole line work. If this isn't well built, nothing downstream is fast or reliable.

Where it lives
Single source of truth, API-accessible
One central system that holds every ready-to-present candidate. No duplicates across spreadsheets, Notion pages, or recruiter heads. Must be queryable by the AI matching tool, the presentation page builder, and the dashboards.
Strong candidate: Airtable. We already use it, it has the MCP integration we run on top of, and it supports the field structure we need without custom code. Open to alternatives if someone has a better proposal.
Field structure
Structured for AI matching
Every candidate record must include the following, machine-readable:
  • Identity: Name, location, country, time zone
  • Role tags: Primary role + adjacent roles (multi-select)
  • Seniority + experience years
  • Salary range: Confirmed, in USD
  • Availability: Status (active / paused) + last-confirmed date
  • Assets: Polished resume URL + intro video URL
  • Tech / tools stack (multi-select)
  • Language proficiency
  • Source channel + sourced-by
  • Lifecycle status: Sourced / Polishing / Ready / Placed / Paused / Inactive
Access model
Who writes, who reads
  • Sourcing — creates the initial record (Sourced status)
  • Candidate Ops — writes polished assets, confirms availability and salary, promotes records to Ready status (this team owns the database)
  • Recruitment / TAs — reads only. Matches against the database, builds presentation pages from the records. Cannot edit candidate data directly.
  • Decision Partners — reads only. Uses match output and interview intelligence to advise the member.
Lifecycle
How candidates enter, refresh, exit
  • Entry: Only Candidate Ops can promote a record to Ready. Promotion requires all required fields filled and assets attached.
  • Refresh: Weekly availability + salary check. Records not refreshed in 30 days auto-flag.
  • Decay: Records not refreshed in 60 days drop to Paused. 90 days → Inactive.
  • Reactivation: Inactive records can re-enter the line via Candidate Ops only.
  • On placement: Status flips to Placed with member + start date. Stays in DB for replacement scenarios.
Recommendation
Build the database in Airtable as the v1. Same base across all four workstreams. Same MCP integration we already use. We can layer a custom front-end later if Airtable's UI becomes the bottleneck, but the data model stays portable.
Risks

Where this could go sideways

Three risks I've already stress-tested. Each one has a concrete mitigation.

01
The handoff tax
If handoffs are sloppy, the integration cost eats the efficiency gains. Today one TA owns the candidate end-to-end. That's slow but seamless.
Mitigation
  • Documented input / output specs per stage
  • Async-first, single shared system of record
  • Stage SLAs (e.g. Candidate Ops processes in 48h)
  • Weekly pipeline review at the stage level
02
Losing the power of surfacing
Today we have huge sourcing volume and we surface fast. The safety stock model means a smaller ready-to-present database. We can't lose surfacing speed.
Mitigation
  • Continuous feed into safety stock from Sourcing
  • Recruitment gets faster (no availability checks)
  • Assault Team fast-tracks urgent / unique roles
  • Watch metric: time-to-first-presentation
03
Can't depend on one person to run it
If the system requires a supply chain brain to operate every day, it isn't built right.
Mitigation
  • Stage leads own throughput per stage
  • We manage the system, not the requests
  • Narrow scope makes onboarding fast
  • No unicorns required at any seat
