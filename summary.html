Executive Summary · 2-min read

Recruitment as a Production Line

Sales & Operations Planning applied to recruitment. Four parallel workstreams. One execution gate. A 30-year-proven model.

The Proposal in One Sentence
Turn recruitment into a real production line where recruiters recruit, salespeople sell, candidate ops polishes, and sourcing fills the funnel — so we present in minutes and scale by managing the system, not every request.
The Framework
We're not inventing this. We're applying it.
Sales & Operations Planning (S&OP) is how serious companies match demand with supply. Five-step cycle: plan demand, plan supply, match the two, execute, review performance. Every modern supply chain runs on it. We've been running recruitment without that discipline. Until now.
Business case · It worked before
Dell's Build-to-Order revolution (1990s)
Dell stopped keeping finished PCs in inventory. Instead, they held safety stock of components and assembled on order. Same logic, different industry — that's exactly what we're proposing for talent.
Inventory: 32 → 4 days
Delivery: 6 weeks → 5 days
Market: #1 PC maker by 2001
The Model
Four stages. One flow. A feedback loop.
Each stage has a clear input, focused activities, and a clean output. The output of one stage becomes the input of the next.
Stage 01
Sourcing
Active pipelines + Assault Team + Strategic sourcing
Stage 02
Candidate Ops
Polished resumes, videos, availability, salary. The safety stock.
Stage 03
Recruitment / TAs
AI matching, presentations, interview prep + reports
Stage 04
Decision Partners
Member strategy, advisory, the close
↻ Stage 4's "next hire identified" feeds back into Stage 1's sourcing strategy. The line keeps feeding itself.
Why It Wins
Speed, visibility, scale
  • Speed without quality tradeoff. A safety stock of pre-vetted candidates means we present in minutes, not days.
  • Bottlenecks become visible. Slow presentations? Candidate Ops. Slow matching? Recruitment. Fix the stage without breaking the line.
  • Right people, right seats. Recruiters recruit. Decision Partners advise and close. Sourcers source. No more square pegs in round holes.
  • AI-ready. Each stage compounds with technology. The structure was built for it.
  • Better candidate experience. A dedicated team polishes profiles, filters out ghosters, and the cream rises.
The Plan
Build in parallel. One execution gate.
All four workstreams advance at the same time. We're ready to run the new model the day Candidate Operations starts delivering. Primary focus: scoping the Decision Partner role well.
  • Sourcing — Mostly in place. Active pipelines for most-requested roles already running. Just needs formal structure.
  • Candidate Operations — To build. New department. Profile: async-first, attention to detail. Build the AI tooling.
  • Recruitment / TAs — Already performing. Add Pod Lead figures + matching, presentation, interview-report tools.
  • Decision Partners — To define. Specific profile (Account Manager + Pod Lead blend). Starting bench: Manuel, Manuela, Feli, Miguel.
The Infrastructure
The ready-to-present database
The safety stock that makes the whole line fast. If this isn't well built, nothing downstream is reliable.
  • Where it lives. Single source of truth, API-accessible. Recommendation: Airtable as v1 (already in use, MCP integration ready).
  • What it holds. Polished resume + video, confirmed availability + salary, role tags, lifecycle status. Machine-readable for AI matching.
  • Who owns it. Candidate Ops writes. Recruitment + Decision Partners read only.
  • How it stays fresh. Weekly availability checks. 30 days flagged, 60 paused, 90 inactive. Reactivation via Candidate Ops only.
Risks & Mitigations
Where it could go sideways
01 · The handoff tax
Mitigation: Documented input/output specs per stage. SLAs. Single source of truth.
02 · Losing surfacing speed
Mitigation: Continuous feed into safety stock. Assault Team fast-tracks urgent.
03 · Can't depend on one person
Mitigation: Stage leads own throughput. We manage the system, not the requests.
What Needs Team Input
Questions still open to brainstorm
  • Org & ownership. Who leads each stage? What's the single shared system of record?
  • Candidate Ops. How much do we automate from day one vs. start manual?
  • Recruitment / TAs. How many Pod Leads / Team Leads? How do we distribute workload?
  • Decision Partners. Comp model — sales-style commissions, hybrid, or something else? Right starting bench?
  • The database. Can interview reports auto-enrich the candidate's profile? Big upside, big design question.
